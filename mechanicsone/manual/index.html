Finding your way around

Every screen is reachable from the list down the left-hand side. There are eighteen of them and they are always in the same order, so a screen you used yesterday is where you left it. Nothing is nested; nothing is behind a menu.

All eighteen are laid out the same way: what you change on the left, what it does in the middle, what it comes to on the right. Drag anything on the left and the picture and the numbers move in the same frame. On a narrow window the three fold into two and then one, and the pairing that survives folding is always slider-next-to-picture — because that pairing is the point.

Every screen opens on a worked set of numbers. Nothing is blank and nothing has to be filled in before the screen does something, so you can always start by dragging what is already there and watching what moves. Each screen's table below lists the value it opens on.

Conventions

  • Units. Everything is held internally in SI and converted only where it is shown. Changing a unit picker moves the number, not just the label.
  • A dash for a unit means the quantity has none. It is a ratio — a length over a length, a stress over a stress — so it is the same number whatever units you measured the two parts in. Slenderness, Poisson's ratio, the spring index, a factor of safety and joint efficiency are all of this kind. Strain is too, which is why it is usually quoted in millionths.
  • Downward is positive for beam deflection, because that is the direction loads act and the direction you would sketch. A load pushing down is entered as a negative intensity.
  • A dash in a readout means there is no answer, not that the answer is zero. It appears when an input is missing or the question has no answer as posed.
  • Refusals are results. A load outside the span or a hole larger than the plate it is cut from produces a sentence explaining why, not a number and not a crash.
  • Choices are never made for you. Where two answers are both defensible, both are shown with the reason beside them. Every screen below has a Where you have to choose heading; if it says there is nothing to choose, there really is nothing.

How the screens connect

Most screens stand alone, but two of them build something the others reuse. This is the one piece of structure worth learning first, because it is what lets you carry a single problem across several screens instead of typing the same section in four times.

  • Beam Diagrams builds the beam — its span, supports and every load on it, with the shear and moment that follow. One later screen uses it without asking you again: Beam Deflection. Change it once and it moves.
  • Section Builder builds the cross-section itself — its outline, area, centroid, second moments and extreme fibres. Seven later screens use it without asking you again: Beam Diagrams, Beam Deflection, Combined Loading, Columns, Bending Stress, Shear Stress, Plastic Bending. Change it once and every one of them moves.

Each screen below says at the top which of these it needs, so you can also just start anywhere and follow the pointer back.

Stress at a Point

Rotate the element and watch every component move together.

Where it is. 1st of 18 in the sidebar. It stands alone: nothing has to be set up before it.

What it is for

Drag the angle and watch the element rotate, the circle point travel, and all three components move together.

σ_x′=(σ_x+σ_y)/2+(σ_x-σ_y)/2 cos 2θ+τ_xy sin 2θ σ_(1,2)=(σ_x+σ_y)/2±√(((σ_x-σ_y)/2)²+τ_xy²)

The plane of the element is not always the plane that fails. Ordering all three principal values decides it.

τ_abs=(σ_max-σ_min)/2 over {σ_1,σ_2,σ_3=0}

The same circle as for stress, provided the shear strain enters halved. That halving is the commonest error in the subject.

ε_x′=(ε_x+ε_y)/2+(ε_x-ε_y)/2 cos 2θ+γ_xy/2 sin 2θ

Three normal components in, three out, plus the split of the stored energy that the distortion criterion depends on.

Where you have to choose

Which maximum shear is being reported?

largest in the plane of the element  |  largest over all planes in three dimensions

For two principal stresses of the same sign these differ by a factor of two, in the unsafe direction. Show both, always.

Stress at a Point, the shear panel: tau max in plane and tau max absolute are shown as a pair, always, with a decision card beneath saying which plane carries the larger.

Do the in-plane principal stresses share a sign?

same sign: the out-of-plane pairing governs and tau equals half the larger  |  opposite signs: the in-plane pairing governs

This is a decision the user must see stated, not a hidden conditional. Getting it wrong under-predicts.

Stress at a Point, the state-of-stress panel: the sigma x, sigma y and sigma z sliders. The decision card in the shear panel names the pairing that governs and changes as the signs of the principal stresses do; the tau max absolute readout beside it changes with it.

Which two-dimensional idealisation applies?

plane stress: the out-of-plane stress vanishes  |  plane strain: the out-of-plane strain vanishes

These are different physical assumptions with different out-of-plane consequences; they must never be mixed on one screen.

Stress at a Point, the state-of-stress panel: the sigma z slider, with the decision card headed which idealisation applies stating plane stress while sigma z is zero, plane strain while the out-of-plane strain is, and neither otherwise. The epsilon z readout in the strain panel is the number the card is reading.

A worked task

A point in a loaded plate is pulled hard one way, pushed the other, and sheared as well. You want the worst tension it sees and the plane that tension acts on.

  1. Type the three in-plane components into the left column.
  2. Read the principal pair and the angle straight off the right column; the circle in the middle redraws as you type.
  3. Drag the angle slider to that angle and watch the shear readout fall to zero — that is the check.
You type
σ_x120.0 MPa
σ_y-40.00 MPa
τ_xy50.00 MPa
It comes back with
σ_1134.3 MPathe larger principal stress
σ_2-54.34 MPathe smaller one
θ_p16.00 degrotate the element by this much and the shear vanishes
τ_max94.34 MPathe largest shear in the plane

The two principal values straddle the pair you typed, and the maximum shear is exactly half their difference. Both are worth checking by hand once.

Where you have to choose

Which sign convention should the diagrams use?

shear positive when it rotates the element clockwise  |  the opposite convention

Two of the four primary texts draw these upside down from each other. Offer the switch; never choose silently.

Beam Diagrams, the conventions panel: the switch reading shear positive clockwise. The shear diagram mirrors about the axis when it is thrown.

Which restraint is released?

the prop reaction  |  the fixing moment

Both are valid and must agree; letting the user switch makes the check a teaching device.

Beam Diagrams, the conventions panel: a two-segment control reading the prop / the built-in end, shown whenever the support chosen above it is indeterminate. The reactions are recomputed from the other release and must agree.

A worked task

A beam sits on two supports and carries a load spread evenly along the whole span. You want the largest bending moment and where it happens.

  1. Leave the support picker on the simply supported case.
  2. Set the span, then add one uniform load covering the whole beam.
  3. The peak is marked on the moment diagram and printed beside it.

Combined Loading

Five actions at once, and the point on the section that suffers most.

Where it is. 5th of 18 in the sidebar. It reads the cross-section itself — its outline, area, centroid, second moments and extreme fibres from the Section Builder screen, so set that up first if you have not.

Axial & Thermal

Bars, temperature, misfit and the gap that changes everything.

Where it is. 18th of 18 in the sidebar. It stands alone: nothing has to be set up before it.

What it is for

One force over one area. Every later screen reduces to this at the last step.

σ=P/A τ_avg=V/A ε=δ/L n=σ_fail/σ_allow

Any two of E, G, K and nu fix the other two. Six inversion paths that must all agree.

G=E/(2(1+ν)) K=E/(3(1-2ν)) e_v=((1-2ν)/E)(σ_x+σ_y+σ_z)

A bar pulled along one axis still carries shear on every other plane. The bridge to the transformation screens.

σ_θ=σ_x cos²θ τ_θ=-(1/2)σ_x sin 2θ

The same weight applied three ways gives three different stresses. Which way is a choice the user must make.

U=P²L/(2AE) nᵢₘₚ=1+√(1+2h/δₛₜ)

Four shapes of the same integral. Each degenerates into the prismatic case, and the degeneracy is where the arithmetic breaks.

δ=PL/(AE) δ_cone=4PL/(π E d_1 d_2) δ_wedge=PL ln (b_2/b_1)/(E t (b_2-b_1)) δ_self=γ L²/(2E)

Equilibrium alone is not enough; compatibility supplies the missing equation.

δ₁=δ₂ and P₁+P₂=P ⟹ σ₁/E₁=σ₂/E₂, σ₁A₁+σ₂A₂=P

A bar that cannot expand carries stress with no load on it at all.

δ_T=α Δ T L σ_restrained=-E α Δ T σ_gap=-E(αΔ T L-g)/L

A geometric discontinuity multiplies the nominal stress. Two shapes have exact closed forms; the chart families do not, and are not guessed at.

σ_θ(a,θ)=σ(1-2 cos 2θ) K_t=3 K_t=1+2a/b=1+2√(a/ρ)

In a cylinder the circumferential stress is exactly twice the longitudinal one, always.

σ_hoop=pr/t σ_long=pr/(2t) σ_sphere=pr/(2t)

Is the fastener in single or double shear?

single: tau = V/A  |  double: tau = V/(2A)

A factor of two, and the commonest slip in the whole subject. Never pick for the user.

How is the load applied?

gradually: factor 1  |  suddenly: factor 2  |  dropped from height h: factor 1+sqrt(1+2h/delta_st)

Three separate sections in every textbook and a factor of two or more between them.
